# Saracen Bridge Analytics — Production, Budget, Insurance and Completion-Bond Readiness

**Version:** V2.0  
**Effective date:** 28 August 2026  
**Status:** Corrected and consolidated internal methodology  
**Use:** Saracen Bridge Film Finance Analyst — film and television greenlight, closing, underwriting and monitoring

> This is an internal analytical and control framework. It is not legal, tax, accounting, insurance, completion-bond or investment advice. Appropriate professional advisers must review specialist matters. Current law, regulation, incentive rules, market terms, insurance, bonding, lender appetite and counterparty strength must be verified for each assessment.

## Controlling application rules

A completion bond from an acceptable guarantor is mandatory for every financed production. Budget, schedule and cashflow must reconcile to a credible cost to complete. Insurance and bond status must be tested by binding evidence, coverage period, conditions, exclusions, endorsements and lender rights; a quote, application or indication does not satisfy the gate.

## Incorporated methodologies

## Production Finance Readiness Checklist

### 1. Purpose
This checklist tests whether the project can credibly move from financing close through production, delivery and repayment without avoidable execution risk.

### 2. Producer capability
Assess:
- completed credits;
- projects at comparable budget;
- delivery record;
- financing record;
- relationship with line producer / production manager;
- ability to manage reporting;
- history of cost overruns or disputes if known;
- local jurisdiction experience.

Do not equate creative producing experience with production finance capability.

### 3. Director
Record:
- attachment status;
- agreement status;
- availability;
- relevant credits;
- experience at proposed scale;
- action / VFX / child / location complexity where relevant;
- schedule implications;
- replacement provisions.

### 4. Cast
For each key role record:
- performer;
- status;
- evidence;
- fee / quote basis;
- availability;
- shooting window;
- insurance;
- travel;
- conditions;
- sales significance.

Budgeting a star at an aspirational quote without evidence should be flagged.

### 5. Key crew
Review:
- line producer / UPM;
- production manager;
- production accountant;
- cinematographer;
- production designer;
- 1st AD;
- VFX supervisor;
- post supervisor;
- stunt coordinator;
- other complexity-specific heads.

### 6. Schedule
Test:
- prep length;
- shooting days;
- company moves;
- location complexity;
- night work;
- stunts;
- water;
- animals;
- children;
- crowd days;
- VFX plates;
- weather exposure;
- travel;
- unit moves;
- turnaround;
- post schedule;
- delivery date.

The schedule and budget must tell the same production story.

### 7. Budget
Confirm inclusion and realism of:
- cast;
- director;
- producers;
- crew;
- fringes;
- payroll taxes;
- equipment;
- locations;
- travel;
- accommodation;
- transport;
- art;
- costume;
- hair and makeup;
- stunts;
- VFX;
- post;
- music;
- legal;
- insurance;
- E&O;
- accounting;
- audit;
- completion;
- delivery;
- financing costs;
- lender fees;
- interest;
- tax incentive fees;
- contingency.

### 8. Contingency
Assess:
- percentage;
- exclusions;
- whether already allocated;
- production complexity;
- completion requirements;
- whether contingency is cash-funded.

A nominal contingency that is already committed elsewhere is not genuine downside protection.

### 9. Cashflow
Test:
- opening cash;
- weekly / monthly spend;
- peak funding;
- equity funding dates;
- debt draw timing;
- tax incentive bridge;
- pre-sale / MG receipts;
- deposits;
- holdbacks;
- delivery payments;
- interest;
- fees;
- reserves;
- final repayment.

A fully financed budget can still fail if the finance arrives too late for the cashflow.

### 10. Locations and jurisdiction
Review:
- country / region;
- production permits;
- local labour;
- currency;
- tax incentive;
- production services;
- travel;
- political / regulatory risk;
- weather;
- security;
- infrastructure.

### 11. VFX and technical complexity
Where material, require:
- VFX breakdown;
- shot count;
- vendor estimates;
- methodology;
- schedule;
- turnover assumptions;
- contingency;
- delivery specifications.

### 12. Post-production
Review:
- edit;
- sound;
- score;
- music licensing;
- grade;
- VFX;
- ADR;
- subtitles;
- dubbing;
- QC;
- mastering;
- archival;
- deliverables.

### 13. Delivery
Identify:
- distributor / platform delivery schedule;
- technical specifications;
- legal deliverables;
- music cue sheets;
- chain-of-title pack;
- E&O;
- captions;
- subtitles;
- localisation;
- artwork / publicity;
- M&E tracks;
- delivery date and cure period.

### 14. Insurance and completion
Assess:
- production package;
- cast;
- equipment;
- workers;
- E&O;
- local requirements;
- exclusions;
- deductible;
- completion bond / guarantor;
- bondable budget;
- bondable schedule;
- exclusions from completion coverage.

### 15. Production readiness rating
#### Green
Core team, budget, schedule, cashflow, insurance and delivery pathway are evidenced and mutually consistent.

#### Amber
Project is credible but material execution items remain conditional or unverified.

#### Red
Budget/schedule mismatch, material cashflow gap, missing production leadership, unrealistic assumptions or no credible completion/delivery pathway.

### 16. Required production gap table
| Issue | Why it matters | Priority | Required action | Evidence to close | Finance impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### 17. Completion test
Before describing a project as production finance ready, the analyst should be able to answer:
1. Who is responsible for physical production?
2. Is the schedule credible?
3. Does the budget support that schedule?
4. Does the cashflow fund the budget when required?
5. Are key dependencies contracted or realistically conditionable?
6. Is insurance adequate?
7. Can the project be delivered to the contracted buyers?
8. What happens if production overruns?

## Film Budget Benchmarking Manual

### 1. Purpose
This manual provides a structured method for assessing whether a film or television budget is commercially proportionate, internally coherent and financeable.

It does not prescribe one correct budget for a genre. Budget reasonableness must be judged against the project’s actual production method, cast, locations, schedule, technical complexity, jurisdiction and delivery requirements.

### 2. Core questions
For every budget review, answer:
1. Does the budget reconcile mathematically?
2. Does it reflect the production schedule?
3. Is the spend proportionate to the project’s likely market?
4. Are material risks funded?
5. Are finance and delivery costs included?
6. Are cost assumptions evidenced?
7. Are there related-party charges or producer fees requiring scrutiny?
8. Does the budget support the proposed incentive assumptions?
9. Does the budget support completion?
10. What lines are most likely to overrun?

### 3. Budget evidence hierarchy
Classify each major assumption as:
- Contracted
- Quoted
- Benchmarked
- Producer estimate
- Assumed
- Unverified

High-value lines should not remain unverified at financing close without a clear reserve or condition.

### 4. Review by cost section
Assess:
- story and rights;
- producers;
- director;
- cast;
- above the line;
- production staff;
- camera;
- grip;
- electric;
- art department;
- sets;
- locations;
- costume;
- hair and makeup;
- sound;
- transport;
- travel;
- accommodation;
- catering;
- stunts;
- special effects;
- VFX;
- post-production;
- music;
- legal;
- accounting;
- audit;
- insurance;
- completion;
- delivery;
- financing;
- contingency.

### 5. Genre and complexity indicators
Do not use genre alone. Adjust for:
- action;
- stunts;
- period setting;
- children;
- animals;
- water;
- night shoots;
- remote locations;
- travel;
- multiple countries;
- crowd scenes;
- VFX intensity;
- specialist vehicles;
- music rights;
- celebrity cast;
- compressed schedule;
- premium-format delivery.

### 6. Budget bands
Use descriptive bands rather than hard-coded universal thresholds unless the user or current market evidence supports specific numbers:
- micro / ultra-low;
- low budget;
- lower-mid;
- mid-budget;
- upper-mid;
- premium independent;
- studio-scale.

The analyst should benchmark within a genuinely comparable production class.

### 7. Above-the-line concentration
Calculate:
- ATL as % of total budget;
- cast as % of total budget;
- producer fees as % of total budget;
- director fee as % of total budget.

Flag where high ATL concentration leaves insufficient resources for physical production.

### 8. Contingency
Assess:
- percentage;
- exclusions;
- whether it is genuinely unallocated;
- whether completion guarantor requires a different level;
- project complexity;
- foreign exchange;
- weather;
- VFX;
- travel.

A contingency that is already allocated is not contingency.

### 9. Finance costs
Check inclusion of:
- lender fee;
- legal;
- interest;
- unused line / commitment fee where applicable;
- monitoring;
- audit;
- tax incentive financing costs;
- currency hedging;
- collection account fees;
- completion fees;
- reserve;
- bank charges.

### 10. Budget-to-schedule test
Examples of inconsistency:
- schedule shows 30 shoot days but crew costs reflect 24;
- cast travel is missing despite location assumptions;
- VFX-heavy script but minimal post/VFX line;
- multi-country shoot but no local production services cost;
- long post schedule but insufficient editorial payroll;
- night work without premiums or transport implications.

### 11. Market proportionality
A budget should be assessed against:
- likely distribution path;
- realistic sales value;
- cast value;
- genre;
- audience;
- comparable productions;
- equity return potential.

If the realistic commercial ceiling is materially below the required capital, recommend:
- budget reduction;
- package enhancement;
- stronger pre-sales;
- more non-dilutive finance;
- alternative jurisdiction;
- staged financing.

### 12. Red flags
Escalate:
- material round-number assumptions;
- key lines unsupported by quotes;
- financing costs omitted;
- no contingency;
- high related-party fees;
- producer deferrals presented as cash;
- under-budgeted post;
- missing delivery;
- incentive calculated on ineligible costs;
- insurance excluded;
- cast fees far below known commercial expectations without evidence;
- crew rates inconsistent with jurisdiction.

### 13. Required output table
| Budget area | Amount | Evidence basis | Benchmark view | Risk | Required action |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|

### 14. Conclusion
State:
- whether the budget is production-feasible;
- whether it is finance-feasible;
- major under-budgeted areas;
- major over-budgeted areas;
- required contingency;
- whether the budget should be locked, revised or rebuilt.

## Completion Bond Underwriting Guide

### 1. Purpose
This guide sets the analytical framework for reviewing completion risk and completion bond arrangements. Under Saracen Bridge policy, an acceptable completion bond is mandatory for every financed production.

Completion guarantees are transaction-specific and must be reviewed by appropriate counsel and specialist advisers.

### 2. Core questions
Assess:
- Has the mandatory completion bond been approved, bound and evidenced?
- Is the project bondable?
- Is the guarantor acceptable?
- What exclusions remain with the producer/lender?
- Is the bond budget consistent with the lender budget?
- Does the bond cover delivery?
- Can the guarantor take over production?
- What lender rights exist?

### 3. Bondability factors
Consider:
- locked script;
- locked budget;
- schedule;
- cast availability;
- director track record;
- line producer;
- production jurisdiction;
- insurance;
- stunts;
- VFX;
- weather;
- children;
- animals;
- travel;
- stage availability;
- technical complexity;
- contingency;
- finance certainty.

### 4. Completion guarantor review
Assess current:
- reputation;
- financial capacity;
- claim history where available;
- jurisdiction;
- ability to step in;
- experience at comparable budget.

### 5. Bond documentation
Review:
- completion agreement;
- guarantee;
- producer undertakings;
- lender agreement;
- interparty / tri-party arrangements;
- takeover rights;
- distribution delivery obligations;
- approved budget;
- approved schedule.

### 6. Key exclusions
Identify:
- force majeure;
- pandemic / communicable disease;
- war;
- civil unrest;
- strikes;
- cast exclusions;
- uninsurable risks;
- VFX exclusions;
- delivery elements outside guarantee;
- finance shortfall;
- producer breach.

### 7. Overcost mechanics
Clarify:
- who funds first;
- use of contingency;
- producer overage obligation;
- guarantor obligation;
- lender obligation;
- thresholds;
- takeover trigger.

### 8. Lender protections
Look for:
- direct agreement;
- notice rights;
- cure rights;
- approval over material changes;
- step-in / takeover recognition;
- delivery obligation;
- assignment.

### 9. Bond fee and reserves
Confirm:
- fee included in budget;
- legal costs;
- required contingency;
- reserves;
- bond exclusions requiring separate funding.

### 10. Red flags
Escalate:
- project described as bonded without an acceptable binder, issued guarantee and executed completion documentation;
- budget differs from bond budget;
- key cast uninsurable;
- guarantor excludes core production risks;
- completion obligation stops before distributor delivery;
- inadequate contingency;
- project materially changes after bond approval.

### 11. Conclusion
State:
- bondability;
- bond status;
- guarantor quality;
- uncovered risks;
- lender protections;
- conditions before reliance.

Bondable, application submitted, indicative approval and bonded are different states. A project may not be classified GREENLIGHT READY by relying on the bond unless the acceptable guarantor, final approved budget and schedule, coverage, exclusions, lender protections and issued or executed bond evidence have been verified.

## Production Insurance Review Guide

### 1. Purpose
This guide provides an underwriting checklist for production insurance as part of finance readiness.

Insurance coverage and exclusions must be confirmed by qualified brokers and counsel.

### 2. Core policies
Review as applicable:
- production package;
- cast;
- negative / media;
- equipment;
- property;
- extra expense;
- workers' compensation / employers' liability;
- public liability;
- auto;
- E&O;
- cyber;
- travel;
- political risk;
- kidnap / ransom;
- specialist stunt / aviation / marine coverage.

### 3. Cast insurance
For key cast:
- insurability;
- exclusions;
- medical;
- pre-existing conditions;
- replacement;
- additional expense;
- age-related restrictions;
- hazardous activities.

### 4. E&O
Confirm:
- coverage period;
- limits;
- deductible;
- title;
- copyright;
- defamation;
- privacy;
- trademark;
- contractual exclusions;
- distributor requirements.

### 5. Lender interests
Confirm:
- lender named where required;
- loss payee;
- additional insured;
- notice of cancellation;
- assignment of proceeds;
- minimum notice period.

### 6. Deductibles
Assess whether deductibles are:
- budgeted;
- cash-funded;
- within contingency;
- material to cost-to-complete.

### 7. Exclusions
Identify:
- communicable disease;
- war;
- terrorism;
- stunts;
- hazardous activities;
- weather;
- cast-specific exclusions;
- countries;
- cyber;
- data;
- equipment.

### 8. Coverage period
Ensure coverage spans:
- prep;
- shoot;
- post;
- delivery where required.

### 9. Claims risk
Assess:
- prior claims;
- claims process;
- broker quality;
- insurer quality;
- jurisdiction;
- response time.

### 10. Red flags
Escalate:
- key cast uninsurable;
- material exclusion not funded;
- policy expires before production completion;
- lender not protected;
- deductible exceeds available contingency;
- E&O absent where distributor requires it.

### 10A. Evidence and binding-status test
An insurance document is not sufficient merely because it exists in the data room. Distinguish:
- application or submission;
- indicative quote;
- accepted quotation;
- binder / cover note;
- issued policy;
- required endorsements and lender loss-payee wording;
- premium paid and policy in force.

GREENLIGHT READY requires evidence that all mandatory cover is bound or otherwise in force for the required period, subject to verified conditions and exclusions. An unaccepted quote does not satisfy the insurance gate.

## 11. Conclusion
State:
- insurance readiness;
- key exclusions;
- lender protections;
- budget adequacy;
- remaining conditions.
