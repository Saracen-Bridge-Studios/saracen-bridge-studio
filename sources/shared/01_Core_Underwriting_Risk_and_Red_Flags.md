# Saracen Bridge Analytics — Core Underwriting, Risk and Red Flags

**Version:** V2.0  
**Effective date:** 28 August 2026  
**Status:** Corrected and consolidated internal methodology  
**Use:** Saracen Bridge Film Finance Analyst — film and television greenlight, closing, underwriting and monitoring

> This is an internal analytical and control framework. It is not legal, tax, accounting, insurance, completion-bond or investment advice. Appropriate professional advisers must review specialist matters. Current law, regulation, incentive rules, market terms, insurance, bonding, lender appetite and counterparty strength must be verified for each assessment.

## Controlling application rules

Apply the formal decision and score mapping in file 00. Risk ratings may override a score. Each finding must identify evidence, consequence, owner, required remediation and objective closing evidence. Missing evidence must be recorded as unable to verify, never assumed favourable.

## Incorporated methodologies

## Film Finance Underwriting Manual

### 1. Purpose
This manual defines the minimum underwriting logic Saracen Bridge Analytics should apply when assessing whether a film or television project is ready for professional debt and equity financing.

The central underwriting question is:

**What must happen next for this project to secure professional debt and equity financing?**

The analyst must distinguish:
1. What is confirmed.
2. What is represented but not evidenced.
3. What is assumed.
4. What is commercially plausible but not yet financeable.
5. What is contractually bankable.
6. What is a matter of professional judgement.

Where evidence is absent, use:

**Unable to verify based on the information supplied.**

### 2. Underwriting principles
#### 2.1 Evidence before optimism
A project is not financeable because a producer expects a sale, believes cast will attach, expects a tax credit, or has verbal investor interest. Finance readiness is based on evidence that can survive lender, investor, legal, accounting and completion scrutiny.

#### 2.2 Contracted value is not automatically bankable value
For every potential collateral source, assess:
- legal enforceability;
- contract execution status;
- counterparty credit quality;
- assignment and security rights;
- payment triggers;
- delivery conditions;
- termination rights;
- currency;
- timing;
- concentration;
- set-off or recoupment rights;
- conditions precedent;
- lender control over collections.

#### 2.3 Development value and financing value are different
A strong screenplay, director, cast aspiration or sales estimate may improve equity attractiveness and future financing potential. It does not itself create debt collateral.

#### 2.4 Finance the receivable, not the headline
For any tax incentive, MG, pre-sale or licence, underwriting must focus on the **net lender-realistic receivable**, after:
- eligibility adjustments;
- non-qualifying expenditure;
- withholding;
- commissions;
- recoupable expenses;
- reserves;
- currency haircuts;
- timing discount;
- legal imperfections;
- counterparty risk.

#### 2.5 No double counting
The same economic value may not appear twice in the capital stack. Examples include:
- a distributor MG counted both as cash equity and debt collateral;
- a tax incentive counted both as a budget reduction and a finance source;
- sales estimates treated as both projected revenue and committed finance.

### 3. Minimum project extraction
For every assessment, record:
- project title;
- genre;
- format and running time / episode count;
- budget and currency;
- production country or countries;
- producer;
- production company / SPV;
- director;
- cast status;
- development stage;
- intended production dates;
- intended delivery date;
- distribution plan;
- current finance raised;
- current finance gap.

Highlight inconsistencies between documents.

### 4. Evidence status
Use the following evidence labels consistently:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Confirmed | Supported by executed or independently verifiable evidence |
| Documented but conditional | Written evidence exists but contains material conditions |
| Represented | Stated by producer or adviser but not independently evidenced |
| Indicative | Estimate, quote, term sheet, LOI or non-binding market signal |
| Assumed | Used for analysis but not evidenced |
| Unverified | Insufficient material to conclude |

### 5. Finance readiness scoring
Default weighting:

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Corporate Structure | 5 |
| Chain of Title | 15 |
| Creative Package | 5 |
| Production Readiness | 10 |
| Budget & Cashflow | 10 |
| Finance Plan | 10 |
| Debt Bankability | 20 |
| Counterparty Quality | 10 |
| Distribution Readiness | 5 |
| Equity Readiness | 5 |
| Risk Management | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Interpretation and formal greenlight mapping:
- **90–100:** Candidate for **GREENLIGHT READY**, but only when all mandatory gates are satisfied and no Critical or unresolved High blocker prevents funding, bonding, insurance, production or delivery.
- **80–89:** Candidate for **CONDITIONAL GREENLIGHT** where the remaining conditions are finite, expressly identified, allocated to responsible owners and capable of satisfaction before the applicable closing or first-draw deadline.
- **55–79:** **HOLD – MATERIAL CLOSING BLOCKERS** unless a higher-risk issue independently requires a Development / Not Yet Closable decision.
- **0–54:** **DEVELOPMENT / NOT YET CLOSABLE**.

The score informs but never determines the formal decision. A score must never conceal a fatal issue. A project with a material unresolved chain-of-title defect, invalid underlying rights, an impossible funding gap, or no executable repayment source may not be described as finance ready regardless of numerical score.

### 6. Gating issues
The following issues normally prevent an institutional finance-ready conclusion until resolved:
- inability to prove ownership or control of material underlying rights;
- unsigned or expired rights required to produce the project;
- no viable production entity or unclear contracting entity;
- budget materially inconsistent with schedule or production method;
- financing sources that do not reconcile to uses;
- material funding gap with no credible source;
- projected debt without identifiable repayment collateral;
- collateral that cannot be assigned or secured;
- material contract termination rights that can eliminate collateral before repayment;
- counterparty credit risk inconsistent with proposed advance;
- tax incentive assumptions not supported by eligibility analysis;
- absence of credible cashflow sufficient to complete production;
- key cast, director or distribution elements described as attached without adequate evidence;
- no acceptable completion bond and practical delivery strategy for a financed production.

### 7. Corporate structure
Assess:
- incorporated production company / SPV;
- jurisdiction;
- ownership;
- directors;
- shareholder structure;
- authority to enter financing;
- intercompany arrangements;
- related-party transactions;
- bank accounts;
- production services structure;
- co-production structure;
- security-granting capacity.

A clean SPV is usually preferable for lender security, collections and transaction clarity. Complex structures must be explained rather than assumed acceptable.

### 8. Chain of title
Review:
- underlying literary or other rights;
- option or purchase agreements;
- writer agreements;
- assignments;
- producer agreements;
- director rights;
- sequel, remake, prequel and derivative rights where relevant;
- music rights where material;
- life rights where relied upon;
- trademark/title issues where material;
- copyright registrations where relevant;
- expiry dates;
- reversion rights;
- cure rights;
- payment obligations.

The analysis must identify the exact link in the rights chain that is missing, conditional, expired or unverified.

### 9. Creative package
Assess creative package only as it affects:
- marketability;
- production feasibility;
- sales value;
- audience positioning;
- distribution interest;
- budget justification;
- equity proposition.

Creative strength is not a substitute for legal or financial readiness.

### 10. Production readiness
Assess:
- producer track record;
- line producer / production management;
- director readiness;
- cast status;
- key crew;
- schedule;
- locations;
- production methodology;
- post-production;
- VFX;
- insurance;
- completion arrangements;
- delivery requirements;
- cashflow;
- contingency;
- foreign exchange exposure.

### 11. Budget and cashflow
Test:
- mathematical reconciliation;
- consistency with schedule;
- realistic crew and cast costs;
- fringes;
- insurance;
- contingency;
- travel;
- locations;
- post;
- VFX;
- music;
- deliverables;
- legal and accounting;
- finance costs;
- interest;
- lender fees;
- completion costs;
- tax incentive fees;
- sales and collection costs;
- reserves.

A budget may be production-feasible but not finance-feasible if financing costs and timing are omitted.

### 12. Finance plan
Every finance source should be classified:

| Source type | Evidence question |
|---|---|
| Equity | Is capital legally committed and funded / callable? |
| Tax incentive | Is eligibility supported and net receivable estimated? |
| MG | Is the agreement executed, assignable and creditworthy? |
| Pre-sale | Is buyer obligation firm and delivery/payment terms bankable? |
| Grant | Is award unconditional or subject to remaining conditions? |
| Producer contribution | Is cash demonstrably available? |
| Gap | Is there sufficient unsold market value after conservative haircuts? |
| Senior debt | What exact collateral repays it? |

### 13. Debt underwriting
For each collateral source record:
- gross amount;
- net lender base;
- contract status;
- counterparty;
- jurisdiction;
- payment date;
- currency;
- assignment;
- security;
- conditions;
- termination risk;
- concentration;
- proposed advance;
- repayment route.

Debt sizing should be based on the lowest credible recovery case, not the producer's expected outcome.

### 14. Equity underwriting
Assess:
- audience proposition;
- comparable performance;
- genre economics;
- budget discipline;
- distribution plan;
- package credibility;
- producer execution capability;
- recoupment waterfall;
- equity position;
- return potential;
- downside;
- upside;
- timing;
- dilution;
- control rights;
- information rights.

A project may be debt-bankable but unattractive equity, or attractive equity but not yet debt-bankable. State which.

### 15. Conditions precedent
Typical categories:
- chain of title completion;
- executed material production agreements;
- confirmed budget and cashflow;
- finance plan fully subscribed;
- tax incentive eligibility evidence;
- assignment of receivables;
- security documents;
- collection account;
- insurance;
- completion arrangements;
- legal opinions;
- corporate approvals;
- delivery materials;
- counterparty notices / acknowledgements;
- funded equity before or pari passu with debt as required.

### 16. Recommendation logic
Use one of the following conclusions where appropriate:
- Proceed to institutional financing.
- Proceed subject to specified conditions precedent.
- Proceed to lender/investor engagement after named remediation.
- Remain in packaging / development pending material evidence.
- Restructure the finance plan before approaching institutional capital.

Every negative finding must include:
1. why it matters;
2. commercial impact;
3. risk level;
4. practical remedy;
5. evidence required to close the issue.

### 17. Confidence
Express confidence in the conclusion as:
- High: core legal, financial and commercial evidence has been reviewed.
- Medium: sufficient evidence exists for a preliminary conclusion but material items remain unverified.
- Low: assessment relies materially on representations, assumptions or incomplete documentation.

## Risk Rating Manual

### 1. Purpose
This manual standardises risk terminology across Saracen Bridge Analytics reports.

### 2. Risk dimensions
Assess each material risk on:
- likelihood;
- impact;
- detectability;
- controllability;
- timing;
- recoverability;
- concentration;
- effect on financeability.

### 3. Likelihood
#### Low
Unlikely based on current evidence.

#### Medium
Plausible and requires active management.

#### High
Likely, recurring or already emerging.

### 4. Impact
#### Low
Limited effect on budget, delivery, rights, repayment or return.

#### Medium
Could cause material cost, delay, dilution or commercial impairment but is manageable.

#### High
Could prevent financing, production, delivery, repayment or materially impair investor recovery.

### 5. Priority rating
#### Critical
A gating issue that can prevent financing, invalidate collateral, stop production or materially impair rights. Requires immediate remediation.

#### High
Material issue that can significantly reduce financeability or cause loss. Must be addressed before closing or first draw unless specifically accepted.

#### Medium
Manageable issue requiring mitigation, monitoring or a condition.

#### Low
Limited issue with modest commercial effect.

### 6. Typical Critical risks
- defective chain of title;
- expired underlying rights;
- no viable funding for material budget gap;
- collateral cannot legally be assigned;
- fraud / misrepresentation concern;
- inability to complete production;
- key licence invalid or terminable before repayment;
- material insolvency risk in repayment counterparty;
- budget and schedule materially irreconcilable.

### 7. Typical High risks
- weak cast evidence where sales depend on cast;
- long-dated incentive with uncertain timing;
- significant FX exposure;
- distributor with limited credit strength;
- aggressive gap assumptions;
- inadequate contingency;
- material VFX complexity without vendor plan;
- equity not fully committed.

### 8. Medium risks
- non-critical delivery uncertainty;
- modest schedule pressure;
- manageable legal drafting issue;
- secondary counterparty concentration;
- limited sensitivity to one territory;
- modest budget line weakness.

### 9. Low risks
- administrative or presentational issues;
- non-material documentation cleanup;
- minor timing questions with buffers;
- low-value contractual imperfections.

### 10. Residual risk
Always distinguish:
- inherent risk;
- mitigation;
- residual risk.

Do not lower risk simply because a recommendation exists. Lower it only if the mitigation is credible and implementable.

### 11. Risk matrix
| Likelihood | Low impact | Medium impact | High impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Low | Low/Medium | Medium |
| Medium | Low/Medium | Medium | High |
| High | Medium | High | Critical/High |

Use professional judgement where a binary matrix would understate a gating issue.

### 12. Risk language
Preferred:
- “Material but remediable.”
- “Financeability is conditional on…”
- “The risk is not currently mitigated.”
- “Residual risk remains high because…”
- “Unable to verify based on the information supplied.”

Avoid:
- “Probably fine.”
- “Should be okay.”
- “Guaranteed.”
- “Risk free.”

### 13. Risk conclusion
For each major risk explain:
1. What can go wrong?
2. Why does it matter?
3. How likely is it?
4. What is the financial / legal / production impact?
5. What mitigation exists?
6. What evidence closes the risk?
7. What residual risk remains?

### 14. Financeability override
Any single Critical risk may override a strong aggregate readiness score. The report should explain this directly.

## Film Finance Red Flag Library

### 1. Purpose
This library provides recurring warning signs that should trigger additional diligence, reduced reliance, restructuring or escalation.

A red flag is not automatically fatal. It is a prompt to identify evidence, consequence and remedy.

### 2. Rights and legal
- option expired;
- option extension not exercised;
- rights held by an individual while financing entity is different;
- writer agreement unsigned;
- assignment missing;
- conflicting rights grants;
- sequel/remake rights assumed but not acquired;
- life rights relied upon without analysis;
- music rights ignored;
- key agreement signed by wrong entity;
- anti-assignment language blocks financing;
- litigation or threatened claim;
- rights term shorter than distribution term.

### 3. Cast and package
- cast described as attached with no agreement;
- LOI expired;
- availability unverified;
- pay-or-play obligation not budgeted;
- cast fee based on an unverified quote;
- sales estimates depend on cast not yet secured;
- director availability conflicts with schedule;
- key creative has termination rights that undermine financing.

### 4. Budget
- contingency below complexity level;
- financing costs omitted;
- bond fee omitted;
- insurance materially understated;
- fringes / payroll omitted;
- VFX under-budgeted;
- post-production compressed unrealistically;
- delivery costs omitted;
- legal / accounting excluded;
- travel/accommodation understated;
- cost report does not reconcile to budget;
- budget assumes deferred costs as if cash-funded.

### 5. Cashflow
- finance arrives after production needs it;
- incentive receipt assumed during production when paid after completion;
- no interest reserve;
- equity scheduled but not committed;
- deposits shown before contract execution;
- no working-capital buffer;
- peak cash deficit not funded.

### 6. Finance plan
- sources do not equal uses;
- same receivable counted twice;
- tax incentive counted both as budget reduction and funding;
- unsigned LOI shown as committed finance;
- equity “soft circled” but presented as closed;
- producer contribution is entirely deferred;
- gap finance fills an excessive proportion of budget;
- lender debt has no clear repayment collateral;
- capital stack depends on speculative sales.

### 7. Distribution and sales
- sales estimate treated as collateral;
- buyer identity unclear;
- distributor has broad termination rights;
- payment subject to discretionary acceptance;
- unsold territories include already sold rights;
- sales agent expenses uncapped;
- no collection account;
- MG net proceeds materially below headline;
- pre-sale payment back-ended beyond debt maturity;
- territory conflicts across agreements.

### 8. Tax incentive
- headline rate applied to total budget;
- non-qualifying costs ignored;
- producer has not checked eligibility;
- incentive cap ignored;
- audit/certification timing omitted;
- tax liability offset ignored;
- receivable cannot be assigned;
- payment timing is overly optimistic;
- currency mismatch not modelled.

### 9. Counterparty
- newly formed distributor with large obligation;
- buyer lacks operating history;
- repeated payment delays;
- material litigation or solvency concern;
- no parent guarantee despite thin subsidiary;
- concentration in one weak buyer;
- producer relies on reputation rather than current evidence.

### 10. Equity
- gross box office presented as investor return;
- waterfall unavailable;
- distributor deductions omitted;
- investor ranks behind producer recoupment;
- return depends on festival breakout;
- no downside case;
- upside case used as base case;
- budget unsupported by realistic market;
- investor governance absent.

### 11. Production
- no experienced line producer;
- schedule too short for script complexity;
- locations unconfirmed;
- VFX methodology absent;
- no acceptable completion bond, only an unbound application or a completion strategy without executed support;
- delivery date conflicts with schedule;
- key permits missing;
- local production rules not assessed;
- significant weather or stunt exposure not budgeted.

### 12. Documentation behaviour
- inconsistent budgets across decks;
- different finance totals in different documents;
- cast status varies by document;
- budget currency changes;
- old versions represented as current;
- material documents referenced but not supplied;
- signature pages missing;
- dates altered without explanation.

### 13. Governance
- related-party fees not disclosed;
- producer controls all collection accounts;
- no reporting obligation;
- no audit rights;
- transaction costs paid to related parties without benchmarking;
- investor / lender approvals undefined.

### 14. Escalation response
For each red flag:
1. identify exact evidence;
2. classify risk;
3. quantify impact where possible;
4. state required remediation;
5. specify evidence needed;
6. decide whether it is:
   - informational;
   - condition precedent;
   - advance-rate reduction;
   - reserve requirement;
   - restructuring issue;
   - financing blocker.

### 15. Prohibited inference
Do not infer a favourable answer merely because a document is missing. Use:

**Unable to verify based on the information supplied.**
