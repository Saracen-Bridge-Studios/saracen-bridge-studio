## Controlling application rules

Use comparables to test market proportionality, budget and revenue assumptions; never treat them as proof of project performance. Select genuinely comparable titles, show source dates, explain differences and apply time weighting. Current market evidence prevails over static examples.

## Incorporated methodologies

## Comparable Film Methodology

### 1. Purpose
This methodology defines how comparable film and television projects should be selected and used in commercial, sales, equity and budget analysis.

A comparable is an analytical tool, not proof that a project will achieve the same result.

### 2. Selection criteria
Consider:
- genre;
- subgenre;
- tone;
- audience;
- language;
- budget;
- production value;
- cast tier;
- director profile;
- source IP;
- production country;
- release model;
- distributor;
- platform;
- territory;
- release year;
- market cycle;
- certification / rating;
- franchise status;
- awards / festival effect;
- marketing scale.

### 3. Relevance grading
#### High relevance
Shares multiple core commercial characteristics and has a reasonably comparable release context.

#### Medium relevance
Useful on one or more dimensions but materially different elsewhere.

#### Low relevance
Provides context only and should not drive valuation.

### 4. Avoid false comparables
Do not select a film solely because:
- plot is similar;
- title sounds similar;
- both have one actor in common;
- both are in the same broad genre;
- one was exceptionally successful.

Outlier hits require special caution.

### 5. Data fields
Where available capture:
- title;
- year;
- genre;
- budget;
- domestic gross;
- international gross;
- worldwide gross;
- distributor;
- platform / release type;
- cast;
- director;
- production territory;
- awards;
- estimated sales;
- source;
- analytical relevance.

### 6. Theatrical comparables
Adjust for:
- screen count;
- P&A;
- distribution scale;
- territory;
- release period;
- market conditions;
- franchise/IP value;
- cast;
- inflation where material.

Gross box office is not producer revenue.

### 7. Streaming comparables
Streaming performance is often opaque. Distinguish:
- officially reported viewing;
- platform charts;
- third-party estimates;
- press reports;
- anecdotal claims.

Do not infer licence value from viewing data without evidence.

### 8. Sales comparables
Where assessing international sales, focus on:
- territory;
- genre;
- cast;
- director;
- budget;
- sales agent;
- timing;
- buyer appetite;
- release model.

### 9. Budget comparables
A budget comparable should help answer:
- Can this type of film be produced credibly at this budget?
- Is the proposed budget excessive or insufficient?
- Is spend concentrated in value-creating elements?

### 10. Time weighting
Give greater weight to recent comparables when the market has materially changed.

Older comparables may remain relevant for:
- evergreen genre behaviour;
- production scale;
- established franchise patterns.

### 11. Outliers
Identify:
- breakout hits;
- franchise films;
- awards-driven results;
- unusually poor releases;
- pandemic-period distortions;
- exceptional marketing support.

Do not use an outlier as a base-case anchor without explanation.

### 12. Comparable set
Prefer a basket of 4–8 credible comparables rather than one headline title.

For each comparable state:
- relevance;
- difference;
- analytical use.

### 13. Source hierarchy
Prefer:
- primary distributor / studio data;
- official box office databases;
- recognised industry trades;
- audited company reporting;
- specialist market databases.

Current market analysis should include reputable industry reporting where relevant, including Deadline Hollywood and The Hollywood Reporter.

### 14. Conclusion rule
Comparable analysis should support a range, not false precision. State:
- what the comp set supports;
- what it does not support;
- major adjustment factors;
- confidence level.
