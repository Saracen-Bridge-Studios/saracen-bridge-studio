# Saracen Bridge Analytics — Debt, Collateral, Counterparties, Lender Terms and Monitoring

**Version:** V2.0  
**Effective date:** 28 August 2026  
**Status:** Corrected and consolidated internal methodology  
**Use:** Saracen Bridge Film Finance Analyst — film and television greenlight, closing, underwriting and monitoring

> This is an internal analytical and control framework. It is not legal, tax, accounting, insurance, completion-bond or investment advice. Appropriate professional advisers must review specialist matters. Current law, regulation, incentive rules, market terms, insurance, bonding, lender appetite and counterparty strength must be verified for each assessment.

## Controlling application rules

Apply the no-double-counting rule, the 20% budget cap on gap finance and evidence-based collateral haircuts. A receivable is bankable only to the extent that the obligor, contract, assignment, payment timing, jurisdiction, deductions, termination rights and security support reliance. Operational RAG reporting is distinct from the formal greenlight decision.

## Incorporated methodologies

## Debt Collateral and Advance Rate Guide

### 1. Purpose
This guide provides an internal starting framework for evaluating film and television receivables as debt collateral.

**Important:** Advance ranges below are internal underwriting starting points, not representations of current lender market pricing. Actual availability and advance levels must be confirmed for each transaction.

### 2. Core formula
For each collateral item:

**Gross contractual value  
less ineligible / non-recoverable amounts  
less priority deductions  
less currency and timing haircuts  
less legal / counterparty risk haircut  
= Underwritten collateral base**

**Underwritten collateral base × approved advance rate = Indicative debt capacity**

Never size debt from gross headline value alone.

### 3. Collateral hierarchy

| Collateral | Indicative internal starting advance | Key gating issues |
|---|---:|---|
| Certified / highly verified government incentive receivable | 80–90% | Eligibility, audit, timing, assignment, jurisdiction |
| Executed MG from strong credit counterparty | 75–90% | Delivery conditions, termination, assignment, set-off |
| Executed pre-sale from established buyer | 60–85% | Buyer credit, territory, delivery, acceptance, currency |
| Broadcaster / platform licence | 65–90% | Credit quality, conditions, delivery, assignability |
| Awarded grant with limited conditions | 60–90% | Revocation, milestones, assignment |
| Contracted brand / sponsorship receivable | 40–75% | Performance conditions, cancellation, brand credit |
| Contracted ancillary licence | 40–75% | Termination, delivery, usage obligations |
| Gap against unsold territories | 0–30% of conservative unsold value | Sales estimates are not collateral; requires deep market analysis |
| Non-binding LOI / indication | 0% | Not enforceable |
| Sales estimate only | 0% | Not contractual |
| Verbal commitment | 0% | Not evidenced |
| Expected equity | 0% debt collateral | Capital source, not receivable |

These bands should be tightened when documentation, counterparty quality or timing is weak.

### 4. Tax incentive receivables
Assess:
- jurisdiction and programme;
- qualifying entity;
- qualifying spend;
- eligible cost categories;
- cultural / production tests;
- cap;
- audit;
- certification;
- filing;
- expected payment timing;
- assignability;
- lender security;
- historical administrative timing;
- FX;
- tax liabilities / offsets;
- recapture risk.

Do not lend against the statutory headline percentage where the project’s effective receivable is lower.

### 5. Minimum guarantees
A minimum guarantee is potentially bankable only if the obligation is sufficiently firm.

Review:
- executed agreement;
- buyer identity;
- parent / guarantor;
- territory and rights;
- amount and currency;
- deposit;
- delivery triggers;
- acceptance;
- termination rights;
- force majeure;
- offsets;
- distribution expenses;
- cross-collateralisation;
- assignment;
- notice of assignment;
- payment destination;
- expiry / longstop.

A non-binding distribution proposal is not an MG.

### 6. Pre-sales
Review:
- executed territory agreement;
- credit standing of buyer;
- distributor track record;
- payment split;
- delivery requirements;
- technical acceptance;
- censorship / classification conditions;
- cast / director conditions;
- completion;
- currency;
- withholding;
- bank charges;
- assignment;
- collection account routing.

If payment is materially conditional on commercial performance rather than delivery, treat the receivable more like contingent revenue than senior debt collateral.

### 7. Platform and broadcaster licences
Key questions:
- Is the licence fee fixed?
- Is payment contingent on acceptance?
- Can the licensee terminate for discretionary reasons?
- Are there broad morality / reputational outs?
- Are deliverables objectively defined?
- Is there a cure period?
- Can lender step-in rights be recognised?
- Is assignment permitted?
- Does the counterparty require direct contracting with a specific entity?

### 8. Grants
Classify:
- awarded and unconditional;
- awarded subject to standard deliverables;
- conditional approval;
- application submitted;
- intended application.

Only the first two categories normally support debt consideration, and only after reviewing revocation and payment conditions.

### 9. Brand and sponsorship contracts
Review:
- campaign deliverables;
- creative approval rights;
- cancellation rights;
- brand-safety provisions;
- payment milestones;
- production placement obligations;
- talent dependencies;
- reimbursement obligations;
- exclusivity;
- assignment.

The more discretionary the sponsor’s approval rights, the lower the collateral quality.

### 10. Gap finance
Gap debt should be treated as structurally junior to contracted receivable lending in risk terms, even where legally senior.

Requirements should include:
- reputable international sales agent;
- territory-by-territory sales estimates;
- conservative unsold value;
- exclusion of already licensed territories;
- distributor fees and expenses;
- collection costs;
- delivery reserves;
- senior debt priority;
- currency haircut;
- sensitivity analysis;
- evidence of meaningful package value.

Do not underwrite against a producer’s global revenue forecast.

### 11. Counterparty grading
Use an internal qualitative grade:

| Grade | Characteristics | Typical treatment |
|---|---|---|
| A | Strong institutional / major credit, high payment confidence | Highest permitted advance subject to contract |
| B | Established and credible independent with sound record | Moderate-high advance with legal controls |
| C | Smaller or less transparent counterparty | Reduced advance, added reserves or guarantees |
| D | Weak, disputed, newly formed or poorly evidenced | Usually non-bankable without enhancement |

Credit grading must be evidence-based and current.

### 12. Contract grading
| Grade | Contract status |
|---|---|
| 1 | Executed, enforceable, assignable, limited termination, objective payment conditions |
| 2 | Executed but with manageable conditions or documentation gaps |
| 3 | Conditional, significant outs, weak assignment or material uncertainty |
| 4 | Non-binding, unsigned, expired or materially deficient |

A Grade 4 contract should not support senior debt.

### 13. Concentration
Apply additional caution where:
- one counterparty represents most repayment;
- one jurisdiction controls most collateral;
- multiple receivables depend on the same delivery event;
- all collateral is exposed to one currency;
- the same legal defect affects several receivables.

### 14. Timing
Debt capacity must be modelled against:
- draw schedule;
- production cashflow;
- collateral maturity;
- delayed payment scenario;
- interest accrual;
- fees;
- reserves;
- repayment date.

A receivable can be high quality but unsuitable for a facility if it is expected too late without sufficient interest reserve.

### 15. Required underwriting table
For every proposed debt source include:

| Field | Required |
|---|---|
| Description | Yes |
| Gross value | Yes |
| Underwritten net value | Yes |
| Contract status | Yes |
| Counterparty | Yes |
| Counterparty grade | Yes |
| Payment timing | Yes |
| Currency | Yes |
| Assignment rights | Yes |
| Termination / offset risk | Yes |
| Proposed advance | Yes |
| Indicative debt amount | Yes |
| Conditions precedent | Yes |
| Key downside | Yes |

### 16. Policy prohibitions
Do not treat the following as senior debt collateral without additional enforceable support:
- sales estimates;
- unsigned LOIs;
- verbal investor commitments;
- hoped-for streamer interest;
- projected box office;
- projected SVOD value;
- non-contracted ancillary rights;
- unawarded tax incentives;
- unverified producer cash;
- speculative unsold value presented as committed revenue.

## Counterparty Assessment Framework

### 1. Purpose
This framework defines how counterparties should be assessed where repayment, distribution, production or execution depends on them.

Counterparty quality must be evaluated using current evidence. Do not permanently label a company as strong or weak based solely on historical reputation.

### 2. Counterparty types
Assess as relevant:
- distributors;
- sales agents;
- broadcasters;
- streamers;
- studios;
- brands;
- incentive administrators;
- production service companies;
- completion guarantors;
- insurers;
- financiers;
- co-producers;
- vendors.

### 3. Credit factors
Consider:
- legal identity;
- ownership;
- parent company;
- jurisdiction;
- financial statements where available;
- profitability;
- liquidity;
- leverage;
- payment history;
- litigation;
- insolvency indicators;
- market reputation;
- years operating;
- management stability;
- concentration;
- external ratings;
- guarantees;
- collateral;
- payment performance.

### 4. Operational factors
Consider:
- ability to perform contract;
- distribution capability;
- territory reach;
- delivery infrastructure;
- track record;
- staffing;
- market access;
- regulatory licences.

### 5. Contractual factors
A strong company can still produce weak collateral if the contract is poorly drafted.

Review:
- payment obligation;
- conditions;
- termination;
- set-off;
- assignment;
- guarantees;
- governing law;
- dispute resolution;
- acceptance;
- cure periods;
- notice;
- force majeure.

### 6. Internal counterparty grades
#### A
Institutional or highly established counterparty with strong evidence of payment capacity and performance.

#### B
Established, credible counterparty with adequate evidence and manageable risk.

#### C
Smaller, newer, opaque or financially weaker counterparty. Reduced reliance required.

#### D
Weak, disputed, insolvent, highly uncertain or inadequately evidenced. Normally unsuitable as core senior repayment source.

### 7. Enhancement
A weaker counterparty may be improved by:
- parent guarantee;
- bank guarantee;
- letter of credit;
- escrow;
- deposit;
- insurance;
- security;
- shorter payment timing;
- reduced advance;
- reserve;
- diversification.

### 8. Concentration
Identify:
- proportion of repayment from largest counterparty;
- correlated counterparties;
- jurisdiction concentration;
- group-company concentration;
- common delivery dependencies.

### 9. Current verification
For material counterparties, verify as appropriate:
- corporate status;
- recent financial health;
- material litigation;
- insolvency;
- ownership changes;
- recent payment concerns;
- market activity.

### 10. Required counterparty table
| Counterparty | Role | Jurisdiction | Evidence | Grade | Exposure | Key risk | Enhancement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|

### 11. Conclusion
State:
- whether counterparty risk supports the proposed advance;
- whether reduced advance is needed;
- whether credit enhancement is required;
- whether diversification is sufficient;
- confidence level.

## Lender Reporting and Cost Monitoring Protocol

### 1. Purpose
This protocol defines the information and controls expected during production when Saracen Bridge Analytics is assessing or monitoring lender exposure.

### 2. Monitoring objectives
Monitoring should establish:
- current cost position;
- remaining cost to complete;
- schedule status;
- use of contingency;
- finance source status;
- covenant compliance;
- collateral changes;
- delivery risk;
- expected repayment timing.

### 3. Standard reporting cadence
Unless the transaction requires more frequent reporting:
- weekly production cost report during principal photography;
- weekly cash position;
- weekly schedule update;
- monthly full cost report during prep/post;
- immediate escalation for material adverse events.

### 4. Core reports
Request:
- cost report;
- variance report;
- cashflow;
- bank statement / controlled account statement;
- commitment report;
- purchase order / AP ageing where relevant;
- payroll summary;
- production schedule;
- daily production reports where needed;
- contingency ledger;
- finance draw schedule;
- incentive qualifying spend report;
- delivery status.

### 5. Cost report fields
The report should distinguish:
- original budget;
- approved changes;
- current budget;
- actual cost;
- committed cost;
- estimate to complete;
- estimated final cost;
- variance;
- contingency remaining.

### 6. Variance thresholds
Use project-specific materiality. Escalate any variance that:
- threatens completion;
- consumes material contingency;
- affects collateral;
- changes delivery timing;
- causes covenant breach;
- requires unapproved equity;
- creates a new financing gap.

### 7. Contingency controls
Monitor:
- opening contingency;
- approved uses;
- pending claims;
- remaining contingency;
- projected contingency at completion.

Contingency should not be used to conceal structural under-budgeting.

### 8. Schedule monitoring
Track:
- planned vs actual shoot days;
- scenes completed;
- major delays;
- cast availability;
- weather losses;
- overtime;
- VFX / second unit;
- reshoots;
- post delivery.

### 9. Draw controls
Each draw should reconcile to:
- eligible costs;
- cash need;
- equity contribution;
- remaining facility;
- CP compliance;
- cost-to-complete test;
- borrowing base where applicable.

### 10. Cost-to-complete test
At each material draw, assess:
- cash available;
- undrawn committed finance;
- remaining budget;
- remaining contingency;
- required reserves;
- expected collateral receipts.

If available committed resources are below the credible cost to complete, escalate immediately.

### 11. Exception reporting
Immediate escalation:
- key cast loss;
- director termination;
- uninsured loss;
- litigation;
- major injury;
- strike / shutdown;
- material schedule overrun;
- material budget overrun;
- incentive eligibility issue;
- buyer default;
- distributor termination;
- fraud concern;
- production company insolvency;
- completion guarantor intervention.

### 12. Lender dashboard
Recommended fields:
| Metric | Current | Prior | Threshold | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Estimated final cost | | | | |
| Contingency remaining | | | | |
| Schedule variance | | | | |
| Cash available | | | | |
| Undrawn committed finance | | | | |
| Incentive estimate | | | | |
| Debt outstanding | | | | |
| Expected repayment date | | | | |

### 13. Completion phase
Before final lender release:
- delivery confirmed;
- final cost report;
- outstanding claims;
- incentive filing;
- receivable assignments active;
- collections routed;
- final legal deliverables;
- E&O active;
- completion obligations satisfied.

### 14. Monitoring conclusion
State:
- **operational monitoring RAG:** Green / Amber / Red;
- current cost-to-complete position;
- key emerging risks;
- remedial actions;
- whether further draws should proceed;
- the current formal greenlight decision under the Governance, Greenlight and Evidence Standard.

Operational RAG is a production-monitoring signal only. It must not be presented as, or substituted for, the formal project greenlight decision.

## Lender Term Sheet Principles

### 1. Purpose
This guide identifies the commercial terms that should be analysed when reviewing or proposing a film finance lender term sheet.

It is not legal drafting.

### 2. Facility
Record:
- borrower;
- facility type;
- amount;
- currency;
- purpose;
- availability period;
- maturity;
- repayment source.

### 3. Pricing
Record and distinguish:
- cash interest;
- PIK interest;
- default interest;
- arrangement fee;
- commitment fee;
- exit fee;
- monitoring fee;
- legal fees;
- break costs;
- minimum interest;
- participation.

Do not compare facilities solely on headline interest rate.

### 4. Borrowing base
If applicable define:
- eligible collateral;
- advance rate;
- concentration limits;
- reserves;
- eligibility tests;
- revaluation;
- ineligible receivables.

### 5. Security
Assess:
- fixed / floating charge;
- copyright;
- receivables;
- bank accounts;
- insurance;
- distribution rights;
- incentive;
- shares;
- guarantees;
- collection account.

### 6. Conditions precedent
Use the transaction-specific CP library and identify which CPs are:
- underwriting gates;
- closing conditions;
- first-draw conditions.

### 7. Draw mechanics
Review:
- minimum draw;
- draw notice;
- supporting evidence;
- cost-to-complete;
- equity funding;
- borrowing base certificate;
- no-default condition.

### 8. Covenants
Potential covenants:
- approved budget;
- approved schedule;
- no material changes;
- no additional debt;
- no new liens;
- reporting;
- cost monitoring;
- completion;
- insurance;
- distribution;
- incentive compliance;
- account control.

### 9. Events of default
Review:
- payment;
- misrepresentation;
- covenant breach;
- insolvency;
- invalid security;
- material contract termination;
- rights defect;
- abandonment;
- failure to complete;
- buyer default;
- material adverse effect if included.

### 10. Cure rights
Assess:
- producer cure;
- equity cure;
- completion guarantor cure;
- lender step-in;
- timing.

### 11. Control rights
Review lender approval / consultation over:
- budget changes;
- schedule changes;
- cast;
- director;
- distribution;
- new debt;
- settlement;
- rights amendments;
- material contracts.

### 12. Economic comparison
When comparing term sheets calculate:
- total fees;
- expected interest;
- effective cost;
- equity dilution / participation;
- reserves;
- legal costs;
- required cash equity;
- maturity risk.

### 13. Red flags
Escalate:
- facility amount exceeds collateral;
- undefined borrowing base;
- broad lender discretion;
- excessive minimum interest;
- hidden exit fee;
- personal guarantee not highlighted;
- producer assumes non-recourse when documents are recourse;
- repayment date precedes realistic receivable collection;
- covenant package inconsistent with production operations.

### 14. Recommendation
State:
- commercial attractiveness;
- key negotiation points;
- unacceptable terms;
- structural issues;
- effective economic cost;
- conditions before acceptance.
