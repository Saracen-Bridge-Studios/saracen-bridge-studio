# Saracen Bridge Analytics — Equity, Capital Stack and Waterfalls

**Version:** V2.0  
**Effective date:** 28 August 2026  
**Status:** Corrected and consolidated internal methodology  
**Use:** Saracen Bridge Film Finance Analyst — film and television greenlight, closing, underwriting and monitoring

> This is an internal analytical and control framework. It is not legal, tax, accounting, insurance, completion-bond or investment advice. Appropriate professional advisers must review specialist matters. Current law, regulation, incentive rules, market terms, insurance, bonding, lender appetite and counterparty strength must be verified for each assessment.

## Controlling application rules

Production equity plus producer risk capital must not exceed 30% of budget. The normal target is 70–80% debt, contracted or non-dilutive finance, subject to genuine collateral and project economics. An equity attractiveness sub-score does not replace the formal greenlight score or decision. Model investor receipts after every senior deduction and under downside, base and credible upside cases.

## Incorporated methodologies

## Equity Investment Committee Framework

### 1. Purpose
This framework defines how Saracen Bridge Analytics should assess whether a film or television project presents a credible institutional or sophisticated private equity investment proposition.

Debt underwriting and equity underwriting must remain distinct. A project may be debt-bankable because repayment is secured against contracted receivables while still offering weak equity economics. Conversely, a project may present attractive upside but remain unsuitable for debt until collateral is created.

### 2. Core equity questions
Every equity review should answer:
1. What is the investment thesis?
2. Why does this project deserve capital at this budget?
3. What protects the downside?
4. What creates upside?
5. What is the investor's position in the recoupment waterfall?
6. What must happen operationally for the base case to be achieved?
7. What happens if sales underperform?
8. How long is capital likely to be outstanding?
9. Which assumptions are evidenced and which are speculative?
10. Is the expected return proportionate to the risk?

### 3. Equity evidence hierarchy
Use:
- Confirmed
- Documented but conditional
- Represented
- Indicative
- Assumed
- Unverified

Do not present projected returns as facts.

### 4. Investment thesis
Assess:
- genre;
- audience;
- concept clarity;
- cast package;
- director;
- producer track record;
- source IP;
- franchise / sequel potential;
- production jurisdiction;
- budget discipline;
- sales position;
- distribution pathway;
- festival / awards strategy where relevant;
- platform relevance;
- market timing;
- competitive differentiation.

The thesis should be expressible in one concise paragraph. If it cannot be stated clearly, the proposition may not yet be investment-ready.

### 5. Budget appropriateness
Ask:
- Is the proposed budget consistent with comparable films?
- Does the package justify the spend?
- Is the budget low enough for the realistic market?
- Is value concentrated in elements that materially improve revenue?
- Is there unnecessary spend that does not improve commercial outcomes?
- Are financing costs included?
- Does contingency match execution risk?

Equity should not be used simply to absorb a structurally over-budgeted production.

### 6. Revenue analysis
Separate:
- contracted revenue;
- probable but uncontracted revenue;
- comparable-derived assumptions;
- speculative upside.

Where relevant, assess:
- theatrical;
- transactional VOD;
- pay television;
- SVOD / AVOD;
- free television;
- international;
- airlines / non-theatrical;
- remake / sequel;
- soundtrack;
- merchandising;
- ancillary rights.

Do not double count rights or revenue windows.

### 7. Recoupment waterfall
Review:
- gross receipts definition;
- collection account;
- distributor fees;
- distributor expenses;
- sales agent commission;
- sales expenses;
- guild residuals;
- senior debt;
- gap debt;
- mezzanine;
- investor recoupment;
- preferred return;
- premium;
- producer corridor;
- backend;
- participations;
- profit definition.

Equity analysis should model receipts to the actual investor position, not simply project-level gross revenue.

### 8. Investor protections
Assess:
- information rights;
- approval rights;
- budget controls;
- related-party restrictions;
- overage protections;
- completion support;
- dilution;
- priority;
- security where available;
- collection account;
- audit rights;
- distribution approval / consultation;
- key-person protections;
- change-of-control protections.

### 9. Return framework
Where data supports modelling, assess:
- multiple on invested capital;
- internal rate of return;
- time to recoupment;
- probability-weighted return;
- downside loss;
- base-case return;
- upside return.

Avoid unsupported precision. Use ranges where appropriate.

### 10. Scenario framework
#### Downside
Use conservative assumptions reflecting:
- failed or weak distribution;
- lower territory sales;
- delivery delay;
- marketing underperformance;
- higher deductions;
- slower collections.

#### Base
Use assumptions grounded in the current package, realistic market evidence and current distribution path.

#### Upside
Use credible, identifiable upside drivers rather than generic optimism.

### 11. Equity readiness scoring
Suggested internal sub-score. This equity score assesses investor attractiveness only; it is not the formal project greenlight score or decision:

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Investment thesis | 15 |
| Team credibility | 10 |
| Budget appropriateness | 15 |
| Distribution strategy | 15 |
| Revenue credibility | 15 |
| Waterfall / investor position | 10 |
| Downside protection | 10 |
| Upside potential | 5 |
| Governance / rights | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Interpretation:
- 85–100: Strong equity proposition subject to final diligence
- 70–84: Investable with identified conditions / price discipline
- 55–69: Speculative; remediation required
- Below 55: Development proposition, not institutional equity-ready

### 12. Red flags
Escalate:
- producer uses gross box office as investor revenue;
- waterfall not supplied;
- distribution fees or expenses omitted;
- equity return relies on awards or festival breakout;
- no downside case;
- recoupment depends on unsold territories at optimistic values;
- investor ranks behind excessive producer or related-party recoupment;
- budget materially exceeds comparable market;
- investor has little or no reporting / audit rights;
- material dilution rights not explained;
- capital requirement exceeds realistic revenue potential.

### 13. Required conclusion
Conclude:
- whether the project presents a credible equity proposition;
- realistic investor profile;
- indicative equity need;
- major return drivers;
- major downside risks;
- required protections;
- conditions before investment;
- confidence level.

## Capital Stack and Waterfall Guide

### 1. Purpose
This guide defines how Saracen Bridge Analytics should structure and assess a film or television capital stack and investor/lender recoupment waterfall.

### 2. Structuring principles
Prioritise:
1. non-dilutive government incentives;
2. executed contracted receivables;
3. senior secured debt;
4. gap or junior debt where justified;
5. equity;
6. producer contribution.

The structure should minimise expensive capital while preserving completion certainty and realistic investor returns.

### 3. Capital stack sources
Classify every source as:
- committed cash;
- contracted receivable;
- debt against receivable;
- conditional finance;
- soft money / incentive;
- grant;
- producer contribution;
- equity;
- gap;
- deferred fee;
- in-kind contribution.

A deferred fee is not cash and should not be treated as cash financing unless the budget presentation explicitly distinguishes it.

### 4. Sources and uses reconciliation
Total sources must equal total uses.

Uses should include:
- production budget;
- financing fees;
- interest;
- lender legal;
- completion / bond fees;
- tax incentive costs;
- reserves;
- contingency;
- delivery;
- collection costs where funded upfront;
- working capital.

### 5. Senior secured debt
Use where repayment is supported by identifiable collateral such as:
- tax incentives;
- MGs;
- pre-sales;
- licences;
- grants;
- contracted receivables.

Senior debt should have:
- clear priority;
- security;
- assignment;
- controlled collections;
- defined maturity;
- covenants;
- draw conditions.

### 6. Gap finance
Gap finance should:
- sit against conservatively underwritten unsold value;
- be sized after deductions;
- be structurally subordinate in risk to contracted-receivable lending;
- include stronger return and controls commensurate with risk.

### 7. Equity
Equity should absorb:
- genuine market risk;
- uncontracted upside/downside;
- development / package risk;
- residual production risk not covered by debt.

Do not use equity to disguise a budget that should first be reduced.

### 8. Producer contribution
Classify as:
- cash;
- verified services;
- deferred fees;
- development spend;
- in-kind.

Do not overstate non-cash contribution as liquid finance.

### 9. Intercreditor considerations
Review:
- ranking;
- payment priority;
- enforcement rights;
- standstill;
- cure rights;
- turnover;
- shared security;
- collection account waterfall;
- voting;
- amendment rights.

### 10. Example stack: tax credit + pre-sales + equity
Illustrative only:

| Source | % of budget | Character |
|---|---:|---|
| Tax incentive bridge | 25% | Senior debt against incentive |
| Pre-sale / MG loan | 45% | Senior / pari passu receivable debt |
| Equity | 25% | Risk capital |
| Producer cash | 5% | Junior / aligned risk capital |

Total equity and producer risk capital is 30%. This reflects the Saracen Bridge ceiling and is not a recommendation for any specific project.

### 11. Example stack: incentive + broadcaster + gap + equity
Illustrative:

| Source | % of budget | Character |
|---|---:|---|
| Incentive | 25% | Soft money / bridged |
| Broadcaster licence loan | 30% | Receivable-backed debt |
| Gap facility | 15% | Junior / higher-risk debt, within the 20% budget cap |
| Equity | 25% | Risk capital |
| Producer contribution | 5% | Junior / aligned risk capital |

Total debt is 70%; total equity and producer risk capital is 30%.

### 12. Waterfall analysis
Model from gross collections through to investor receipts.

Typical order:
1. collection account fees;
2. taxes / withholding;
3. approved distribution / sales deductions;
4. senior lender principal and interest;
5. junior / gap lender;
6. equity recoupment;
7. preferred return / premium;
8. producer corridor or participations;
9. residual profit split.

Actual priority must follow contract documents.

### 13. Waterfall red flags
Escalate:
- producer backend paid before investor recoupment;
- uncapped sales expenses;
- cross-collateralisation not understood;
- distributor fees above market without justification;
- debt priority unclear;
- tax credit bridge treated as permanent finance;
- equity shown as committed without evidence;
- gap facility sized on gross unsold sales;
- double counting of receivables;
- producer contribution is entirely deferred and presented as cash.

### 14. Required output
For every finance structure recommendation include:
- sources and uses;
- percentage by source;
- debt amount;
- equity amount;
- collateral;
- ranking;
- pricing assumptions if supplied or verified;
- maturity assumptions;
- conditions precedent;
- funding sequence;
- repayment waterfall;
- principal risks;
- alternative structure if the preferred structure fails.

### 15. Funding sequence
The analyst should identify:
- who funds first;
- whether equity is fully funded or pro rata;
- whether senior debt requires equity first;
- timing of incentive bridge;
- deposits from buyers;
- reserves;
- working capital;
- final delivery payments;
- debt repayment date.

A capital stack is not operationally viable until funding timing reconciles with the production cashflow.

## Equity Waterfall Worked Examples

### 1. Purpose
These examples demonstrate how to analyse investor recoupment using simplified illustrative assumptions.

They are not forecasts and should never replace project-specific agreements.

### 2. Example A: simple equity recoupment
Assumptions:
- equity investment: $2.0m;
- no senior debt remaining at exploitation stage;
- net producer receipts after distribution deductions: $3.0m;
- investor recoups 100% of investment first;
- remaining profit split 50/50 investor/producer.

Calculation:
1. Net receipts: $3.0m
2. Equity recoupment: $2.0m
3. Remaining profit: $1.0m
4. Investor share of profit: $0.5m
5. Investor total receipts: $2.5m
6. MOIC: 1.25x

This example does not account for timing, so IRR is not stated.

### 3. Example B: preferred return
Assumptions:
- equity: $3.0m;
- 20% preferred return payable after capital recoupment;
- remaining profit split 50/50;
- net receipts: $5.0m.

Calculation:
1. Capital recoupment: $3.0m
2. Preferred return: $0.6m
3. Remaining: $1.4m
4. Investor profit share: $0.7m
5. Investor total receipts: $4.3m
6. MOIC: 1.43x

### 4. Example C: senior debt before equity
Assumptions:
- net collections: $6.0m;
- senior debt repayment: $1.5m;
- equity: $3.0m;
- equity recoups next;
- remaining profit split 50/50.

Calculation:
1. Net collections: $6.0m
2. Senior debt: $1.5m
3. Remaining: $4.5m
4. Equity recoupment: $3.0m
5. Remaining profit: $1.5m
6. Investor share: $0.75m
7. Investor receipts: $3.75m
8. MOIC: 1.25x

### 5. Example D: downside case
Assumptions:
- equity: $3.0m;
- net receipts after senior deductions: $1.8m.

Result:
- investor recovers $1.8m;
- unrecovered capital: $1.2m;
- MOIC: 0.60x.

A waterfall should always show capital-loss cases where plausible.

### 6. Example E: producer corridor
Assumptions:
- equity recoups first;
- after recoupment, producer receives 10% corridor;
- remaining profit split 50/50.

The analyst must specify whether the corridor is:
- before investor preferred return;
- after preferred return;
- before or after participant deductions.

Small drafting differences materially change economics.

### 7. Example F: distributor deductions
Assumptions:
- gross receipts: $10.0m;
- distributor fee: 20%;
- expenses: $1.0m;
- collection fee: $0.1m.

Producer-level net:
1. Gross receipts: $10.0m
2. Distributor fee: $2.0m
3. Expenses: $1.0m
4. Collection: $0.1m
5. Net: $6.9m

Do not model investor returns from the $10.0m gross number.

### 8. Modelling rules
For every waterfall:
- identify gross receipts;
- deduct distribution fees;
- deduct approved expenses;
- deduct collection costs;
- repay debt;
- recoup equity;
- apply preferred return;
- apply corridors;
- split profits;
- deduct participations where required.

### 9. Scenario presentation
Use:
- downside;
- base;
- upside.

For each show:
- net receipts;
- investor receipts;
- MOIC;
- time to recoupment;
- IRR only if timing assumptions are defined.

### 10. Red flags
Escalate:
- gross box office used as investor receipts;
- undefined “net profits”;
- uncapped distributor expenses;
- producer corridor ahead of investor capital;
- participations omitted;
- equity dilution not modelled;
- debt repayment omitted;
- current waterfall not provided.

### 11. Conclusion
State:
- investor recoupment position;
- breakeven level;
- downside loss;
- base-case return;
- upside sensitivity;
- principal deduction risks.
