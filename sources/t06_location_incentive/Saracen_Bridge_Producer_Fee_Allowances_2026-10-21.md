# Producer-fee allowance for incentive eligibility (Saracen Bridge)

Saracen Bridge Ltd's note on the maximum producer or executive-producer fee that can count as qualifying expenditure for a film incentive, across the countries in the budgeting and scheduling rate cards. Sources checked 21 October 2026. Received 4 November 2026. The Studio applies it in Location & Incentives (Producer and EP fees).

I’ve interpreted this as the maximum producer or executive-producer fee that can count as qualifying expenditure for a film incentive.

Across the 13 countries in your budgeting and scheduling rate cards, there is no single percentage of the total budget. Some schemes cap producer fees, some pool them with other above-the-line costs, and others assess the actual services performed.

The figures below concern eligible expenditure, before applying the rebate or tax-credit rate. They reflect sources checked on 21 October 2026.

“Confirm” means I could not establish a reliable numerical maximum for that particular route; it should not be treated as uncapped.

For Europe, the national rules below cover the rate-card regions unless a separate regional scheme is identified.

UK — England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland
No fixed producer-specific percentage identified for AVEC.
Claim only the qualifying production-related portion. HMRC requires allocation between development and qualifying production phases; a fee covering the whole project is not automatically wholly eligible. GOV.UK
Ireland — all regions
Normally 10% or 15% of the global budget, shared between producer, co-producer, EP fees and production/corporate overheads.
Revenue normally accepts 15% where eligible expenditure exceeds 80% of the global budget, and 10% where it is below 80%. These are acceptance benchmarks: higher amounts can be substantiated. Exactly 80% needs clarification because the guidance’s wording does not resolve that boundary. revenue.ie
Germany — all Länder, DFFF I
For budgets above €500,000: 5% of production costs excluding the producer fee, capped at €250,000.
Smaller-budget limits are €15,000 for budgets up to €300,000 and €25,000 for budgets above €300,000–€500,000. Exceptions require approval. This is a recognised-cost ceiling; German territorial eligibility still applies. FFA en
Germany — DFFF II service-production route
5% of the production costs for which the service producer is responsible, excluding its fee; maximum €350,000.
This concerns the production-service-provider fee, not an unrestricted additional foreign EP fee. Regional funds have separate conditions.
Hungary — all regions
4% of the Hungarian budget, pooled across producer fees.
Includes line, co-, executive and other producers. NFI also excludes purchases of goods/services from the foreign producer or co-producer, so a foreign company’s fee needs particular scrutiny. Nemzeti filmiroda
Italy — all regions, incoming/international tax-credit route
0% for producer fees and general company overheads under this route’s eligible-cost rules.
The separate 30% overall ATL ceiling does not make producer fees eligible. Domestic production credits and regional grants require separate assessment. italyformovies.com
Malta — Malta, Gozo and Comino: individual ATL remuneration
Shared ATL allowance of €1 million or 30% of total Malta eligible spend, under the applicable approved option.
Shared with directors, cast and stunts; line producers are excluded from this ATL category. Only Malta-attributable work qualifies. The guidelines also cap the rebate generated by ATL expenditure at €5 million. Confirm the option with the Commission before budgeting it. stargatestudios.com.mt
Malta — local production-service/co-production company fee
Lower of €100,000 or 20% of final actual Malta spend.
This is a separate fee category from the individual producer’s ATL remuneration. stargatestudios.com.mt
Spain — national recognised-cost framework, including mainland regions, Balearics and Canaries
Executive-producer remuneration: 5% of the film’s recognised realisation cost under ICAA cost-recognition rules.
Do not automatically apply this as the foreign-production tax-credit allowance. The Article 36.1/36.2 route and the fee’s classification must be checked. A higher Canary Islands incentive rate does not itself increase the producer-fee allowance. spainfilmcommission.com
Spain — Navarra, Article 65.1 route
Executive-producer remuneration up to the higher of 10% of production cost or €100,000, limited to the actual qualifying fee.
This is specific to that route; it should not be copied automatically into Navarra’s foreign-service-production calculation. lexnavarra.navarra.es
Spain — Basque Country: Bizkaia, Gipuzkoa and Álava
Confirm separately for each provincial tax route.
I have not verified a universal producer-fee percentage applicable across these three regimes.
France — national TRIP, across rate-card regions
Confirm the fee classification; no blanket producer-fee percentage verified.
The rules identify eligible production payroll and services, with territorial and nationality/residency conditions. They do not justify inserting an automatic percentage for a foreign producer’s corporate fee. bofip.impots.gouv.fr
Czech Republic — all regions
No fixed producer-specific percentage verified.
Actual qualifying Czech expenditure must be established. Fundraising activities, certain development costs, unpaid own contributions and remuneration for statutory company-office duties are excluded. Czech Film Commission
For Australia and New Zealand, the choice of national incentive route and production start date matters.

Australia — NSW, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia, Tasmania, ACT and Northern Territory: Producer Offset
For non-documentaries, 20% of total film expenditure is the shared ATL/development ceiling in the published guidance checked.
Producers share this allowance with other covered ATL expenditure. Documentaries are exempt from this cap. The announced removal should not be assumed effective without checking the applicable enacted rules. screenaustralia.gov.au
Australia — same states/territories: Location Offset
No fixed individual EP percentage identified. Corporate production-company/service fees are excluded as such.
An individual EP’s actual services in Australia may qualify where they concern making the film. Fees solely for financing or distribution are excluded. Separately identifiable qualifying services can be assessed individually. arts.gov.au
Australia — state/territory grants and top-ups
Confirm against the particular programme and approved budget.
The federal Producer Offset’s 20% figure is not a universal producer-fee allowance for every state grant.
New Zealand — North and South Islands/all regions: international rebate
Former 20% ATL cap removed for productions starting principal photography/eligible PDV from 1 January 2026.
This permits assessment of actual eligible expenditure, not an arbitrary budget percentage. Non-resident non-cast personnel must generally work in New Zealand for at least 14 days in aggregate. Rebate For International Productions
For Canada, B+C means production plus post-production costs, excluding the ATL and other budget sections.

The federal CRA’s 10% × actual B+C producer-fee reference applies to specified related-party, ownership and financier-associated fees. CRA expressly says it is not a maximum: higher fees can be justified; arm’s-length contractual fees are assessed differently. Provincial eligibility remains a separate calculation. Canada.ca

British Columbia — Vancouver, regional BC and Vancouver Island
BC expressly refers applicants to the CRA producer-fee policy above. Apply the qualifying BC labour rules; 10% of B+C is not a hard provincial budget cap. Province of British Columbia
Ontario — GTA, regional and northern Ontario
Use the federal benchmark where applicable, but confirm the provincial fee allowance and labour/service allocation under the selected Ontario credit.
Manitoba — Winnipeg and regional Manitoba
Confirm for the labour-based or cost-of-production route selected. Do not treat the federal 10% benchmark as a universal provincial ceiling.
Québec — Montréal, Québec City and regional Québec: production-services credit
0% for a non-Québec-resident producer’s remuneration under the relevant producer provision. Qualifying producer remuneration requires the individual to be Québec resident when providing the services. No separate fixed percentage verified for eligible resident fees. sodec.gouv.qc.ca
Alberta — Calgary, Edmonton and regional Alberta
10% of actual B+C is a general reasonableness reference, rather than an absolute maximum; higher fees require support. alberta.ca
Saskatchewan
Producer fees normally 10% of B+C; 15% for qualifying documentaries below C$2.5 million. Corporate overhead has its own corresponding allowance. Exceptions are discretionary. creativesask.ca
Nova Scotia — Halifax and regional areas
Eligible compensation is limited by C$150,000 of incentive per individual, not C$150,000 of fee. Thus the fee ceiling is C$150,000 ÷ applicable incentive rate: at 25%, C$600,000. Resident-labour and additional-role restrictions also apply. cch.novascotia.ca
Newfoundland and Labrador
Confirm separately for the labour-based and all-spend routes. No reliable producer-specific numerical ceiling established in this review.
Yukon — Media Production Fund
Combined producer fees and corporate overhead: 30% of B+C for budgets up to C$500,000; above that, 20% of B+C, capped at C$2 million, under the published business guidelines. Treaty co-productions use the Canadian share. yukon.ca
Northwest Territories
Confirm the approved territorial rebate cost schedule. No numerical producer-fee maximum verified.
Nunavut
Confirm the approved Nunavut-spend allocation. Producer residency, ownership, control and reasonable remuneration are reviewed; no universal percentage verified. Nunavut Film Development Corporation
For the US jurisdictions in the cards, dollar figures below are US dollars. A per-person compensation ceiling generally covers the individual’s combined roles, rather than a separate allowance for each credit.

California — Los Angeles and outside the zone
0% for ATL producer/EP fees. Limited qualifying BTL work by someone also credited as a producer is a separate exception, capped at $100,000 for the specified roles. film.ca.gov
New York — NYC and upstate
Fees for no more than two producers, within a shared ATL pool of 40% of all other qualified production costs. This is not 40% of the total budget. NYSenate.gov
New Jersey — inside and outside the 30-mile zone
General route: $750,000 per individual. Studio/film-lease routes have additional ATL allowances. Certain qualifying partner productions can also include apportioned out-of-state producer fees within a 7.5% pool shared with rights fees and production insurance. njeda.gov
Georgia
$500,000 per person for W-2 compensation. The same salary ceiling does not apply to qualifying loan-out/other eligible payment arrangements; Georgia work and tax requirements still apply. georgia.org
Louisiana
Shared ATL ceiling of 40% of Louisiana expenditure. The former $3 million individual compensation limit was removed for the applicable post-July 2025 regime. admin.opportunitylouisiana.gov
Illinois
$500,000 per individual, also constrained by 40% of Illinois production spending for all ATL, and 12% for related-party ATL in aggregate. Limited exceptions exist to the 40% rule. REVENUE
Texas
Published programme guidance: $1 million total compensation per Texas-resident worker per project. Non-resident producer wages do not qualify under that resident-payroll provision. gov.texas.gov
North Carolina
First $1 million per individual; excess compensation is excluded. www3.ncleg.gov
South Carolina
Payroll provision excludes an employee’s salary where it is $1 million or more. This is an exclusion threshold, not simply a rebate on the first $1 million. law.justia.com
Oklahoma
Producer fees share the 25% ATL ceiling on statutory qualifying production expenditure. Relevant Oklahoma taxpayer/loan-out requirements apply. oscn.net
Kentucky
First $1 million per ATL employee, resident or non-resident, subject to qualifying Kentucky services. law.justia.com
Oregon
OPIF rules exclude compensation paid to an individual receiving more than $1 million. Treat this as a potential whole-payment exclusion, not merely a cap on the excess. Payment of rebates
Washington
0% for non-resident ATL producers. Resident qualifying wages can be considered; no separate producer percentage established. washingtonfilmworks.org
Puerto Rico
Producer fees up to 10% of total Puerto Rico production expenditure, restricted to Puerto Rico-resident producers, with payment and tax evidence. docs.pr.gov
Massachusetts
Route-dependent. The payroll-credit exclusion for compensation of $1 million or more must be distinguished from the production-expense credit, which can have different treatment. Confirm before assigning a maximum. Mass.gov
Montana
Do not assume a currently available award: the state has paused new certification applications and says the first available credit is in 2031. Existing certifications need separate review. Montana Department of Commerce
New Mexico
Confirm resident/non-resident status and ordinary versus Film Partner treatment. I have not verified a single current maximum applicable to both routes.
Pennsylvania
Confirm producer-specific treatment. The commonly quoted principal-cast ceiling should not be used as a producer-fee allowance.
Utah
Confirm current individual compensation treatment in the approved incentive agreement; no current numerical maximum verified here.
Colorado
Confirm the approved qualifying compensation and fee allocation. No producer-specific percentage verified.
Ohio
Confirm qualifying Ohio services and fee classification. No producer-specific percentage verified.
Arizona
Confirm qualifying Arizona production labour and fee classification. No producer-specific percentage verified.
Hawaii — Oʻahu and neighbour islands
Confirm qualifying producer compensation. Different island incentive rates do not themselves establish a different producer-fee allowance.
For your budget model, calculate the eligible producer fee first, then express it as a percentage of the worldwide budget. For example, a €10 million film with a €5 million Hungarian budget has a €200,000 pooled producer-fee ceiling under the 4% rule—equivalent to 2% of the worldwide budget, before checking the payee’s eligibility.

Where the cap is shared with other ATL costs or overheads, subtract those qualifying costs before calculating the remaining producer-fee allowance. A contractual EP fee can exceed the eligible amount; the excess simply should not be included in the incentive calculation. Have the local incentive accountant confirm the selected route and any entries marked “confirm” before locking the finance plan.
